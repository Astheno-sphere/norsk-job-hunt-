## 1. Call before you apply (her headline hack)
Logic: Norwegian hiring is partly personal; the contact person and phone number in an ad are an invitation, not decoration. A 3 to 5 minute call makes you a person before your CV is opened.
- Step 1, introduce: name, the role you saw, that it fits your experience, where you live.
- Step 2, real questions. Her four: do you take candidates from abroad; is it worth applying; what matters most in this role; how is the work organised.
- Step 3, reference the call in the letter: thank them for the conversation and say it made you more motivated to apply.
- Write three sentences down before calling. Recruiters talk to internationals all the time.

**Tiger adaptation:** he is in Norway, so skip the moving line. Replace "do you take candidates from abroad" with the honest sponsorship/permit question when relevant. Use the answer to "what matters most" as the letter's L2 pain, in their words.

## 2. Silence after applying
- Many Norwegian firms do not open applications until after the søknadsfrist. Give it 1 to 2 weeks after the deadline.
- Then call the contact person, do not just email. Ask how many applied and who went to interview.
- Got a no: call and ask why they chose the other candidate and what made them stronger. Managers repost roles and remember callers.
- Keep applying meanwhile; months is normal for everyone ("ting tar tid").

## 3. Five interview questions you will always get
Rule behind every answer: start from who they are looking for, then find that person in your own experience. Not from who you are.
1. Tell me about yourself: who you are, experience, why you are looking now, plus one active hobby (hiking, skiing).
2. Describe yourself in a few words: use the adjectives from their ad, each backed by one real example.
3. A conflict at work: safest is unclear responsibility; you noticed, talked it through, split tasks. No names, no villains.
4. Hard colleagues: describe a type, never a person (people who only see problems).
5. Automation and new systems: positive; tech takes the repetitive work, people check and decide; plus one story of learning a system.
- Salary: past pay is private. Ask their range first. If pushed, give a range slightly above target with "rundt".

## 4. Networking
- Norway is a small market; managers ask "does anyone know someone". The goal is visibility, not a big network.
- Three moves: call the contact person; connect with people in your field on LinkedIn and actually write; tell everyone you are looking.
- Messages must be specific: you work with X in Norway, five minutes for tips on getting into the field.

## 5. Work permit: the job offer decides
Case: a client lost 18 months because a job offer only said the title. UDI reads exactly what is written and does not infer.
A strong offer states: daily tasks, not just title; why the job needs your exact education; salary and hours at Norwegian standard; start date and contract type.
**Tiger adaptation:** if an employer offers sponsorship, check the offer letter against these four before it goes to UDI.

## 6. Culture
Short emails are respect; leaving at 4 is normal; first names; silence in meetings is thinking; "bra jobba" is real praise; connection happens at lunch, not after-work.

## 7. Market data she cites
NAV Bedriftsundersøkelsen 2026: about 34,000 workers short; biggest in health, industry (mechanics, energy fitters, welders), construction; regions Vestland, Rogaland, Nordland. Shortage does not mean anyone gets hired; authorisation, recognised qualifications and Norwegian still gate.
