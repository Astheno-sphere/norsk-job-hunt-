## Tactics (ranked by fit to our situation)

### 1. Call or write the kontaktperson before applying (NAV/EURES)
Many ads name a contact person. Contact them before applying to test your fit against the requirements. If no reply and you believe you fit, follow up; it can produce CV feedback. **New to our flow.** This is the cheapest warm channel there is: it turns a cold portal into a named person.

### 2. Hidden market is the majority (Hale, Myrnes)
Both cite roughly 70% of jobs never advertised. The figure is unverified (no study named), but it matches our own funnel data. Channels named: events, volunteering, Tekna (engineers), Chamber of Commerce, alumni, LinkedIn direct to hiring managers, NAV career counselling, MESH / The Conduit / Diversify meetups.

### 3. Follow up, always (Thorsdottir)
In the interview, ask when you will hear back and put that date in the calendar. If you did not ask, wait five working days, then send a short LinkedIn message. Silence is not rejection; some companies return months later (McClenahan).

### 4. Commitment signals (Thorsdottir, Størli)
Say you plan to stay, that you are learning Norwegian, and why you are in Norway. This directly counters the flight-risk read our red team keeps finding.

### 5. Three-part bullet (Duong)
What you did, the specific detail, why you did it. Cut bullets that are not strong enough. Be ready to be questioned on every tool listed.

### 6. Reduce perceived risk early (Duong)
Share only essential personal details at first; more as rapport builds. International firms (Aize) strip nationality, marital status, ID and photo.

### 7. Read the company's style first (Hale)
Traditional Norwegian firm or international one? Adjust how much local norm to adopt. Formal at first in interviews, then match the interviewer.

### 8. Cover letter: only if it adds (Thorsdottir) vs always (Ellis)
Thorsdottir: optional, skip if it repeats the CV; use it for motivation and something personal. Ellis: never send a CV without a tailored letter. Resolution: always send one when the ad allows; it must never restate the CV (already ANIMA's CV vs letter rule).

### 9. Interview conduct (Størli, Thorsdottir)
Punctual. First names. Low hierarchy, high trust, work-life balance. Bring questions about the job; suggest improvements as "how about trying X", not as criticism. Never criticise Norway or Norwegians (McClenahan).

### 10. Format (Ellis, Duong, Mikiewicz)
Two pages is normal and acceptable. Airy, clear timeline, no glossy design outside design roles. Key qualifications block at the top, tailored to the ad. One application per job, no standard letter (EURES golden rule).

## Agrees with our doctrine (confidence up)
Warm over cold, tailoring every application, no exaggeration, honesty about weaknesses, Janteloven restraint, learning Norwegian as a signal, persistence through silence.
